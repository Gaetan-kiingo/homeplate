1. Overview
1.1 Project Summary
1.1.1 Purpose, Scope, and Objectives
This Software Project Management Plan (SPMP) governs the delivery of Homeplate v1.0, a two-sided dinner-marketplace application, over a ten-week academic term by a three-person team. Its purpose is to translate the baselined SRS (v3.2) and the architecture decisions already recorded (ADR-001–006) into a concrete work plan, schedule, resource assignment, and risk register the team can be held to at PDR, CDR, and the final presentation. Scope is limited to the v1.0 feature set defined in SRS §1.2 (discovery, booking, reviews, messaging, safety alerts, moderation, data-lifecycle/privacy). Payments, AI-assisted listing generation, and identity verification are out of scope and deferred to v2.0. The objective is a working, demo-able booking loop that satisfies the SRS's functional and non-functional requirements and is independently verifiable against the test protocol in SRS §4, without depending on a live commercial event (per the proposal's risk mitigation).
1.1.2 Assumptions and Constraints
	•	Each of the three team members contributes at most 20 hours/week to this project.
	•	The team's effective active window is ≈ 8 weeks (7/17–9/12), not the full ≈ 10-week class term; the estimate in §5.1.1 is based on that narrower window.
	•	SRS v3.2 is the frozen requirements baseline; changes follow the change process in §5.3.1 and are re-baselined only at CDR. A revised SRS is submitted alongside this SPMP on 8/9/2026.
	•	No project budget exists; all tools and services used are free tier (PostgreSQL, Redis, GitHub, Google Drive, Canvas, AWS/GCP free tier, Google Maps/Places API free tier).
	•	Payments, multi-factor authentication, and identity verification are out of scope for v1.0 (deferred to v2.0 per SRS §2.4) and are not planned for in this SPMP.
	•	PDR (Aug 1), the SPMP/SRS submission (Aug 9), CDR (Aug 22), and the Final presentation (Sep 12) are fixed, non-negotiable course milestones, all in 2026.
	•	The professor is the sole reviewing stakeholder and grader (see §4.1).
	•	The professor does not have access to the team's Google Drive; all graded deliverables (this SPMP, the SRS, decks, and weekly stand-up reports) are submitted to the professor through Canvas. Google Drive is used only for the team's own internal collaboration and version-tracking (§7.1, §7.3).
	•	Lucya Chuang and Gaetan Rieben are based in Southern California; Nam Tran is based in the northern Central Valley. All three share the Pacific time zone, but meeting in person as a full team is impractical, so all collaboration is remote, over Discord (chat/voice). See §4.2.

Claude and Gemini were also used during Weeks 1–4 for documentation and diagram drafting (proposal, SRS, UML, ADRs), each verified or corrected by the team before submission (see the Week 3–4 stand-up reports).

5. Managerial Process Plans
5.1 Start-up Plan
5.1.1 Estimation Plan
Estimation is experience-based, not algorithmic (e.g., COCOMO), because the team has no calibrated LOC/productivity history of its own and COCOMO's inputs (size estimates, a validated cost-driver set) would themselves be guesses at this scale — an algorithmic estimate would just hide the same uncertainty behind more decimal places. Instead, the team is anchoring on its own recent, real data point: actual hours spent producing the proposal, SRS v3.2, and the six ADRs, all within the compressed window since the professor assigned the team on 7/17/2026 (§1.1.2, §1.2). Reasoning:
	•	Active window: the capacity is scoped to the 7/17–9/12 window — ≈ 8 weeks — rather than the full term.
	•	Nominal capacity: 3 members × 20 hrs/week × 8 weeks ≈ 480 person-hours.
	•	Effective capacity: applying the same ≈70% utilization factor observed while producing the requirements/architecture deliverables in this window (once class time, cross-team coordination, and documentation overhead are subtracted) gives ≈336 person-hours of productive engineering time.
	•	Phase allocation (experience-based, and compressed to reflect that PDR fell only ≈two weeks after the team formed): 10% design finalization/setup, already largely covered by the proposal/SRS/ADR work (≈ 34 hrs), 50% core implementation (≈ 168 hrs), 25% integration/testing/polish (≈ 84 hrs), 15% PM/documentation/review overhead — higher than a typical split, to account for the weekly stand-up reporting cadence (§5.3.5) — spread across the active window (≈ 50 hrs).
This estimate is treated as uncertain by design: it is re-checked at CDR (§1.2, §5.3.2), roughly five of the eight active weeks in, against the deliverable-status trend in the weekly stand-up reports (§5.3.5–§5.3.6) — the team does not log hours per activity, so this is a schedule check, not a burn-rate check — and the risk register (§5.4, R-05) carries the consequence of the estimate being wrong.

5.3 Control Plan
5.3.1 Requirements Control Plan
SRS v3.2 is the frozen baseline. Any proposed requirement change is logged as a GitHub issue, reviewed by the full team, and either accepted (with the SRS revised and re-versioned, as in its existing revision history) or deferred to v2.0. No requirement changes are accepted after CDR without PM sign-off.
5.3.2 Schedule Control Plan
The PM checks WA-status against §5.2.2 weekly. CDR (Aug 22) is the formal re-baselining point described in §1.2; if a work activity is behind at that point, the lowest-priority backlog items (e.g., review/messaging polish) are cut before the core booking loop (WA-3) is touched.
5.3.3 Budget Control Plan
N/A — no monetary budget exists to control (§5.2.4). Free-tier API usage (Maps/Places, SendGrid, FCM) is monitored informally against documented rate limits so no paid tier is triggered accidentally.
5.3.4 Quality Control Plan
Every merge requires peer review (§7.4); the test suite (WA-11) against SRS §4 is the quality gate before a feature is considered done. See §7.4 for AI-generated-code-specific review requirements.
5.3.5 Reporting Plan
Every Sunday, the team submits a stand-up report to the professor through Canvas (there is no TA), covering the accomplishments since the last report, the plan for the following week, blockers/risks/help needed, each member's contributions (done and next), the status of each outstanding deliverable, and a brief note on where AI tools helped and how the output was verified. This is the team's primary recurring reporting mechanism; the PDR, CDR, and Final reviews (§7.5) are the larger, milestone-scale checkpoints. Nam Tran (PM) drafts the report on the team's Google Drive from the team's remote check-in (§4.2), then submits it through Canvas.
5.3.6 Metrics Collection Plan
The weekly stand-up report (§5.3.5) tracks deliverable status — each report's status section is checked against §5.2.2 to determine whether a work activity is on schedule, ahead of schedule, or behind schedule. The §5.1.1 estimate is therefore validated at CDR against this schedule-position trend, not against actual hours, which is a real limitation the team accepts given its size; no additional tooling is introduced solely to close that gap.

6. Technical Process Plans
6.1 Process Model
The team uses a blended process: plan-driven at the milestone level, and agile at the execution level. The three fixed course gates (PDR, CDR, Final) are non-negotiable, so the overall shape of the plan is plan-driven — the schedule in §5.2.2 is organized as three sequential phases bounded by those gates, not as an open-ended backlog. Within each phase, however, the team works in short, informal Scrum-like iterations (roughly weekly check-ins, a shared task board, and continuous integration via GitHub Actions), because the requirements are relatively well understood (SRS v3.2 is already baselined) but the implementation details benefit from iteration and peer review. This mirrors how the team already operated during the requirements/architecture phase: a plan-driven document sequence (proposal → SRS → driver analysis → architecture → ADRs) produced through iterative peer review at each step.
6.2 Methods, Tools, and Techniques
	•	Method: incremental development within the phased schedule in §5.2.2.
	•	Client: React Native (iOS/Android) plus a React web experience for host onboarding.
	•	Server: Node.js/Express, PostgreSQL (source of truth), Redis (sessions/cache).
	•	Tools: GitHub + GitHub Actions (CI/CD) for source code, Google Drive for the team's internal shared documentation, Canvas for submitting graded deliverables (§7.1), AWS/GCP free tier, AI coding assistants (Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot), Discord for the team's remote coordination (§4.2).
	•	Technique: peer code review before merge (§7.4); weekly remote check-ins plus the Sunday stand-up report (§5.3.5).
6.3 Infrastructure Plan
Hosting runs on AWS/GCP free-tier instances with GitHub Actions for CI/CD, per the proposal's technical approach. No dedicated servers or paid infrastructure are provisioned for the academic term.
6.4 Product Acceptance Plan
Acceptance is defined as passing the verification protocol already specified in SRS §4 (functional, reliability, security, and performance tests — RT-01/02, ST-01–06, IT-03, LT-01/02) and receiving the professor's sign-off at the Final presentation (§4.1).
7. Supporting Process Plans
7.1 Configuration Management Plan
Source code is version-controlled in a single GitHub repository; changes to the main branch require a pull request and at least one peer review (§7.4). All documents — the SRS, this SPMP, presentation decks, and weekly stand-up reports — are drafted and version-tracked on the team's Google Drive, which is for internal team use only. Each graded deliverable is exported and submitted separately through Canvas (§4.1) on its due date. The SRS and this SPMP follow the same dated-revision convention already used for the SRS's version history (e.g., v3.2).
7.2 Verification and Validation Plan
Verification and validation follow the protocol already defined in SRS §4 (functional test cases per FR, RT-01/02 for reliability, ST-01–06 for security/privacy, IT-03 for moderation accuracy, LT-01/02 for performance); this SPMP does not duplicate that detail and instead schedules its execution as WA-11 in §5.2.1–§5.2.2.
7.3 Documentation Plan
Nam Tran (Documentation lead, §4.3) maintains the SPMP, the SRS, and any design notes; Lucya Chuang supports presentation-facing documentation (PDR/CDR/Final decks). Drafts live on the team's shared Google Drive (§7.1); the professor only ever sees the version submitted through Canvas on each deliverable's due date, including the weekly stand-up reports (§5.3.5).
7.4 Quality Assurance Plan
Gaetan Rieben (QA lead, §4.3) owns test execution against SRS §4. Every pull request requires at least one peer review before merge; this applies without exception to AI-assisted code (R-07–R-09 in §5.4). Security-relevant code paths (auth, input handling) are additionally checked against the specific NFR/ST test they implement before being considered done.
7.5 Reviews and Audits
Three presentations gate the schedule: PDR (Aug 1, 2026), CDR (Aug 22, 2026), and the Final presentation (Sep 12, 2026); this SPMP and a revised SRS are also submitted as a milestone deliverable on Aug 9, 2026. All of these, plus the weekly Sunday stand-up report (§5.3.5), go through Canvas, since the professor does not have access to the team's Google Drive (§7.1). Internally, the team also requires peer code review before every merge (§7.4, §7.1).
7.6 Problem Resolution Plan
Issues (bugs, blockers, reviewer feedback) are logged as GitHub issues and triaged by the PM within 48 hours; anything that threatens a milestone is escalated at the next weekly check-in and handled per §5.3.2 and R-10 in the risk register.
7.7 Subcontractor Management Plan
N/A — the team uses no subcontractors; all work is performed by the three members in §4.3.
